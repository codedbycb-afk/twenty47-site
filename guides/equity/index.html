For loan officers EQUITY

The past borrower check-in so the next call they take is yours.

Your past borrowers hear from servicers, lenders and lead buyers all year. Most of those calls are about a product. Yours should be about them. Here's when to reach out, what to say, and what to keep out of a text.

Keep this out of texts

No rates, payments or loan terms in a text or post. A specific rate, payment, down payment or term in an ad can trigger extra federal disclosure rules. Keep numbers for the call, and use your company's approved language. Check anything new with your compliance team first.
  • Text only borrowers who agreed to hear from you, and honor every opt out right away.
  • Don't promise savings. You don't know yet. That's what the review is for.

1The yearly mortgage review

Once a year, around their closing date. This is the main touch. Everything else supports it.

Text
Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Company]. It's been [a year / X years] since we closed on [street]. I do a free 10 minute review with past clients once a year: where your loan stands, what your home is likely worth now, and whether anything makes sense to change. Want me to send a couple of times?

On the call

  1. Start with them. "How's the house? Anything changed for you this year?" Job, family, plans to move, plans to renovate.
  2. Show the equity picture. Rough home value from recent sales near them, minus what they owe. Say plainly that it's an estimate.
  3. Name their options, with no push. Stay as is, take cash out for a project, refinance if it ever makes sense, or plan a move.
  4. Close with the rate watch. "Want me to tell you if the numbers ever line up for you? I'll only reach out if it's worth your time."
  5. Log it. What they said, what they're planning, and when to follow up.

2The rate watch follow up

Only for borrowers who said yes on the review call. This is the text that beats the servicer to them.

Text
[Name], you asked me to tell you if the numbers ever lined up for you. They might be close. Do you have 10 minutes this week so I can run it properly? If it doesn't make sense, I'll tell you that too.

3The life event check in

When they mentioned a renovation, a baby, a new job or a move on the review call, follow up around that date.

Text
Hey [Name], you mentioned [the kitchen / the baby / the move] last time we talked. How's it going? If any of it changes your plans for the house, I'm happy to walk through options. No rush.

4The equity note

When homes on their street sell. Short, about the house, not the loan.

Text
[Name], a home near you on [street] just sold. Sales like that can change what your home is worth. Want me to send a rough estimate of where your equity sits now? Just an estimate, nothing to sign.

5The thank you after a referral

Text
[Name], [Referral's first name] reached out and said you sent them. Thank you. That means a lot. I'll take good care of them.

Check your company's rules before you send any gift. Settlement service referrals have their own federal rules.

The calendar

  • Closing anniversary: yearly review text
  • Two weeks later, no reply: one short follow up, then wait until next year
  • Rate watch list: text only when the numbers are worth a call
  • Life event dates from the review call
  • Home sales on their street: equity note, at most twice a year
