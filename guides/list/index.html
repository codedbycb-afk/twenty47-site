5 reasons to text a past client and the exact messages to send.

The typical Realtor got half of their 2025 business from repeat clients (28%) and referrals from past clients (22%), according to the NAR 2026 Member Profile. Most agents still go quiet after closing. Here are five reasons to reach out that aren't "just checking in," with texts you can copy.

Before you send anything

  • Text only people who gave you their number and would expect to hear from you. If someone asks you to stop, stop and note it.
  • Send from your own phone, one person at a time. These read like a person because they are.
  • Fill in every [bracket]. A text with their street name in it gets read. A text that could go to anyone gets ignored.
  • No ask in the first text. Give something first. The referrals come from being the agent who stayed in touch.

1Their home anniversary

The one date every past client cares about. Send it on the closing date each year.

Text
Hey [Name], it's [Your name]. One year ago today you got the keys to [street]. Hope the house has been good to you. Anything you've been meaning to fix or change? Happy to send you the contractors my clients like.
Year 2 and after
[Name], [number] years in [street] today. Congrats. If you ever wonder what the house would sell for now, I can pull the sales on your street. No pressure, just say the word.

2A house on their street sold

People always want to know what the neighbor's house went for. You have the answer before anyone else.

Text
Hey [Name], a house on [street] just sold. It's [beds] beds, similar to yours. Want me to send you what it went for and how long it took?

If they say yes, send the number and one line on what it means for them. That reply is the conversation.

3A vendor they'll need

Every homeowner needs a roofer, a plumber or an HVAC person eventually. Be the one they ask.

Spring
[Name], quick one. A few clients asked who to call for [gutters / roof checks / AC tune ups] this spring. I keep a short list of people who showed up on time and didn't overcharge. Want it?
Fall
Hey [Name], furnace season. If you need someone to look at yours before it gets cold, I've got a guy my clients like. Want his number?

4Something you remembered about them

Keep one note per client: a kid starting school, a new job, a dog's name, the reason they moved. Use it once a year.

Text
[Name], I just drove past [place they mentioned] and thought of you. How's [the new job / Maya's first year at school / the dog]?

No mention of real estate at all. That's the point.

5Ask for their help, once a year

After you've given something, it's fine to ask. Make it specific and easy to answer.

Text
Hey [Name], I'm helping a couple who wants to get into [their neighborhood] before spring. If you hear of anyone on your street thinking about selling, would you send them my way? Either way, hope you're doing well.

How to keep it going

The texts are easy. Remembering to send them is the hard part. One sheet fixes most of it:

  • Name, phone, address, closing date
  • One personal note (kids, job, pets, why they moved)
  • Last time you texted, and what about
  • A calendar reminder on every closing date

Aim for four or five touches a year per client. Anniversary, one sale on their street, one vendor tip, one personal note, one ask.
