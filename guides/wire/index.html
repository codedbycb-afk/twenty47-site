For real estate agents WIRE

The wire warning text and the fake seller checklist.

In 2026, 59% of title firms in ALTA's survey reported at least one seller impersonation attempt in the prior year, up from 28% in 2024. Buyers get targeted too: the FBI's 2025 report describes a couple who wired over $449,000 at closing after an email posing as their attorneys. Here's what to send your buyers, what to do if a wire goes wrong, and how to spot a seller who isn't the owner.

Part 1. The buyer warning

In Ohio, a closing agent can only treat cash, checks and cashier's checks as good funds up to $10,000 combined (ORC 1349.21). Above that, the money has to arrive electronically, which for most buyers means a wire. Send this text three times: when you go under contract, at clear to close, and the day before closing.

Text to buyers
Before you send any money for closing, read this. Criminals fake emails from title companies and agents to steal closing funds. Your title company will never change its wiring instructions during your deal. 1. Call [title company] to confirm the wiring instructions before you send anything. Use [phone number] or a number you look up yourself. Never use a number or link from an email. 2. Ask your bank to confirm the name on the receiving account before the wire goes out. 3. After you send it, call the title company again to confirm they got it. If you get any message changing the instructions, don't send. Call me first.

Part 2. If a wire went to the wrong place

Minutes matter. ALTA says catching it within 24 hours gives you the best chance of getting the money back.
  1. Call the sending bank's fraud department now. Ask them to send a recall of the wire because of fraud, and to start the FBI's Financial Fraud Kill Chain.
  2. Call the receiving bank's fraud department. Tell them a recall is coming and ask them to freeze the account.
  3. File a complaint at ic3.gov with the full wire details: account, amount, date and the emails, including email headers.
  4. Call your local police and the FBI field office.
  5. Warn everyone on the deal by phone, using numbers you already trust: buyer, seller, agents, lender, title, attorney.
  6. Call the sending bank again to confirm the recall went out.
Text to everyone on the deal
Important: we've had a wire fraud attempt on [address]. From now until closing, don't act on any email about money or wiring instructions. We'll confirm everything by phone, using numbers we already have for each other.

Part 3. The fake seller checklist

Fake sellers go after property where the real owner isn't around: vacant land, homes with no mortgage, absentee owners, and owners who have died. Check every listing that fits that pattern.

Red flags from the FBI (June 2026 warning)

  • Talks only by email, text or internet phone numbers, and won't meet, with excuses like illness or travel
  • Pushes to close fast, or offers to sell below market value
  • Knows little about the property, has no survey or tax documents, or the details don't line up
  • Wants the money wired abroad, to an account in another name, or somewhere unrelated to the property
  • Deed notarized in another country, or other unusual paperwork

What title firms see most (ALTA survey)

  • Seller avoids meetings and calls
  • Seller asks for a mail-away signing or wants to choose the notary
  • Cash buyer, and the property is owned free and clear
  • Price below market
  • Seller and notary in different states

Before you sign a listing that fits the pattern

  • Meet the seller in person or on a live video call with their photo ID on screen
  • Check the name against the county's ownership and tax records
  • Mail a letter to the address on the tax record and ask the owner to confirm the sale. The FBI suggests a certified letter.
  • Ask questions only the owner would know: when they bought it, what's on the land, who pays the taxes
  • Tell the title company up front that you have doubts. They have ID verification tools.
Text to a seller you can't verify
Thanks for reaching out about [address]. Before I list any property, I meet the owner in person or on a video call with a photo ID. It protects you as much as anyone, since fake sellers are going after vacant land and free and clear homes right now. What time works this week?

A real owner will make time. A fake one usually goes quiet.
