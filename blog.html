The first business to answer wins. Period.

Here's what actually happens when someone needs a plumber, a printer, or a table for eight: they contact two or three businesses at once, and they book with whoever responds first. Not the cheapest. Not the best-reviewed. The fastest.

A lead answered inside five minutes is dramatically more likely to become a customer than one answered an hour later — and most businesses take hours. Nights and weekends? Straight to voicemail, which for most customers means straight to your competitor.

The fix isn't hiring more front-desk staff. It's a system that answers every call, text, DM, and form in seconds — quotes, answers questions, books the appointment — while your team does the actual work. That's the first thing we install for almost every client, because it's the fastest math in the building.

Your old customer list is a pile of money.

Every established business is sitting on hundreds — often thousands — of past customers who liked the work and simply drifted. Nobody called them. Nobody texted. They didn't leave; they were just never invited back.

The math is lopsided: a past customer already trusts you, so win-back campaigns convert at rates cold ads never touch, at almost zero cost. Reactivating even a small slice of a dormant list routinely produces the biggest single revenue jump a business sees all year.

This is why it's our recommended first move in nearly every engagement: it needs nothing new — no ads, no content, no risk — just the list you already own, worked properly, in your voice, booking into your calendar.

Most AI is a toy. Here's what actually pays.

MIT found that roughly 95% of company AI efforts produce zero measurable return. Not because AI doesn't work — because businesses buy tools instead of fixing processes. A chatbot nobody talks to. A subscription nobody opens.

The 5% that win all do the same unglamorous thing: they find one expensive, repetitive process — slow lead response, an untouched customer list, hours of manual scheduling — put AI on exactly that, and measure the dollars. Then, and only then, they scale to the next process.

That's the entire Twenty47 method. We audit first, put a number on the leak, fix the worst one, prove it on a dashboard, and only scale what pays for itself. Boring, measurable, and it's why our stuff ships while everyone else's AI collects dust.
